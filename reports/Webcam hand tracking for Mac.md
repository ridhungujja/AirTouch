# Build a calmer air trackpad, not a magic wand

The "AirTouch" that runs on a MacBook Air M4 today is almost certainly the **paid airtouchapp.com app** (macOS 14+, Apple Silicon only). Two free open-source Mac repos also use the name: NathN07/AirTouch (Swift, Apple Vision) and KartBala07/AirTouch (Python, MediaPipe). The better-known **Neural Lab AirTouch**, which does emulate touch, runs only on Windows, Linux and Android, and its macOS version is still "on the way" with no date. The most mature free Mac baseline is **Pawvis**, an MIT-licensed Swift app built on Apple Vision. No project called "A-Canvas" could be confirmed; the closest match is SkyCanvas, which does webcam air-drawing plus Stable Diffusion/ControlNet styling. Users' complaints have been the same for 13 years, from Leap Motion to today's GitHub virtual mice: tools that won't install, jitter, accidental clicks (the "Midas touch" problem), arm fatigue, lighting sensitivity and macOS permission confusion. These products failed when they tried to replace the mouse for a raised arm. They worked when the hand stayed low and a small pinch confirmed an action, which is Vision Pro's model. A much better Mac version is therefore a native Swift menu-bar app. It should run on-device hand pose with a swappable engine, use an explicit engage/clutch state, relative pointer motion with acceleration, a 1€-filtered cursor kept separate from a lightly filtered pinch detector with hysteresis, and rich visual and audio feedback in a click-through overlay. It should be signed with a stable Developer ID, request permissions just in time, and ship with situational modes (Draw, Present, Media, Accessibility) rather than promise to be a mouse.

## Five products share the AirTouch name, and only three run on your Mac

The name is crowded, and the user's description ("cursor and touch-like events") together with the mention of the Neural Lab blog suggests two products may have been conflated. **Neural Lab's AirTouch** is the one that emulates mouse, keyboard and touch so that host apps such as Unity or TouchDesigner need no code changes ([Sony Spatial Reality Display docs](https://sony.net/Products/Developer-Spatial-Reality-display/en/tips/air-touch)). It launched at CES 2025 for **Android 11+, Windows 10+ and Linux** ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/computing/accessories/neural-labs-airtouch-brings-gesture-control-to-windows-and-android-devices-with-just-a-webcam-180031750.html)), and its 2026 blog and homepage still list macOS as "on the way" ([Neural Lab blog](https://neural-lab.com/blog/best-gesture-control-software); [neural-lab.com](https://neural-lab.com/)). It costs roughly **$29.99/month or $299/year** per device, with a 7-day trial that requires a card ([Neural Lab pricing](https://neural-lab.com/pricing)). Even its CEO concedes it is "not as good as a mouse or touch screen yet" ([Comstocks](https://comstocksmag.com/web-only/startup-month-neural-lab)). On a Mac it would need a Windows VM, and webcam passthrough makes that impractical.

The commercial Mac app promises cursor movement by hand, **pinch to click, two-finger scroll, a fist that opens Spotlight, 10 built-in gestures**, and custom gestures bound to shortcuts, scripts or apps. It requires **macOS 14 Sonoma on Apple Silicon** ([airtouchapp.com](https://airtouchapp.com/)). It also appears on a vibe-coding showcase, which points to a recent, AI-assisted indie build ([vibecode.net](https://vibecode.net/project/airtouch-airtouchapp)). NathN07/AirTouch is a Vision-based "virtual air trackpad" with a floating HUD, middle-finger pinch for right-click, pinch-hold to drag, multi-finger flicks for Spaces and Mission Control, and a peace sign to pause. It needs Accessibility re-granted after every rebuild ([GitHub](https://github.com/NathN07/AirTouch)). KartBala07/AirTouch adds a forward "poke" to click and asks for Camera, Accessibility *and* Screen Recording permissions ([GitHub](https://github.com/KartBala07/AirTouch)).

Beyond the AirTouch name, **Pawvis** is the most developed free Mac tool. It uses 21-joint Vision hand pose on-device, works with built-in, USB or Continuity Camera, supports dwell click and user-trained custom gestures, and has a two-hand wave to stop ([pawvis.app](https://pawvis.app/); [GitHub](https://github.com/alexandriax/pawvis)). Its own site admits it gets **flaky in dim or backlit rooms** and that continuous per-frame inference costs CPU and power ([pawvis.app](https://pawvis.app/)). **Conductor** is another Swift/Vision menu-bar app, with a "hold open hand still to take control" engage gesture and two-hand pinch zoom ([GitHub](https://github.com/NikAtNight/conductor)). Apple's own options are partial. **Head Pointer** moves the pointer with the head, not the hands ([Apple support](https://support.apple.com/guide/mac-help/unac899/mac)). Sonoma's video **Reactions** prove that Apple runs on-device hand-gesture recognition system-wide, but they only trigger effects ([MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/how-to/trigger-reaction-effects-facetime-mac)). Ultraleap now has a native Apple Silicon build, but it needs its own IR hardware ([Ultraleap](https://www.ultraleap.com/downloads/leap-motion-controller-2)).

For air drawing, "A-Canvas" stays unconfirmed. A Hack Club Stardance page (#19169) surfaced for the query but could not be opened ([Stardance](https://stardance.hackclub.com/projects/19169)). **SkyCanvas** matches the description best: a webcam gesture canvas with a FastAPI backend that turns sketches into images through Stable Diffusion plus ControlNet on PyTorch ([GitHub](https://github.com/SteveTM-git/skycanvas)). Dozens of MediaPipe/OpenCV "Air Canvas" repos also exist, such as AirCanvas-AI with shape recognition and undo ([GitHub](https://github.com/antara33/AirCanvas-AI)) and a browser version aimed at video meetings ([GitHub](https://github.com/robert-mcdermott/aircanvas)). They draw inside an OpenCV window rather than across the whole system. Many older ones also break on current installs, because MediaPipe removed the legacy `mp.solutions.hands` API in favour of the Tasks HandLandmarker ([so-you-knock](https://github.com/so-you-knock/gesture-controlled-virtual-mouse)).

## Thirteen years of complaints converge on the same five failures

In the DIY world, the loudest complaint is that **the software never runs**. On the most-starred webcam virtual-mouse repo (about 700–880 stars, depending on the snapshot), most visible issues are mediapipe and OpenCV version errors, plus **at least six separate "No output" reports** ([GitHub issues](https://github.com/Viral-Doshi/Gesture-Controlled-Virtual-Mouse/issues?q=is%3Aissue)). NonMouse, which supports macOS, shows the same pattern with pip and PyObjC failures, and also reports camera frame rates **dropping to about 5 Hz on macOS Monterey** ([NonMouse issues](https://github.com/takeyamayuki/NonMouse/issues?q=is%3Aissue)). A signed, packaged Mac app removes this whole class of problem.

Once the software runs, the complaints concern how it feels to use. **Jitter and imprecision** come first. One academic summary reports **59% accuracy for webcam cursor control against 99% for a mouse**; the specific paper behind this figure could not be verified ([academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/86561308/Mouse_Control_Using_Hand_Gestures)). An SFU study found that un-instrumented in-air pointing reached **less than 75% of mouse throughput**, and blamed latency, click detection and tracking quality ([SFU](https://vvise.iat.sfu.ca/user/data/papers/inairpointing.pdf)). The most candid recent macOS project admits that "very small targets are harder to hit" ([Bhavyashah20/gesture-control](https://github.com/Bhavyashah20/gesture-control)). **False activation** is the second problem. HCI literature defines the Midas touch problem as "normal body movements … mistakenly treated as input" ([Univ. of Glasgow](http://eprints.gla.ac.uk/147499/8/147499.pdf)). A Product Hunt commenter on Pawvis asked specifically about accidental triggers during video calls ([Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/pawvis)). The same macOS project treats false triggers as its main risk: its acceptance test requires ordinary hand movement near the camera to produce "zero intents of any kind" ([Bhavyashah20](https://github.com/Bhavyashah20/gesture-control)).

**Fatigue** is the third problem and the oldest. Alphr wrote that even perfect hardware could not overcome "the sheer arm-aching awkwardness of navigating your PC by gesture" ([Alphr](https://www.alphr.com/peripherals/32310/leap-motion-controller-review/)). Leap Motion's own blog admitted that new users "elevate their entire arm in non-ergonomic ways" ([Leap Motion blog](https://www.leapmotiontechnology.com/blog-sub/taking-motion-control-ergonomics-beyond-minority-report)). HCI researchers built a metric for it, Consumed Endurance ([UManitoba](https://hci.cs.umanitoba.ca/projects-and-research/details/ce)). The fourth problem is **lighting and framing**. Detection "degrades in dim or strongly backlit rooms" ([Bhavyashah20](https://github.com/Bhavyashah20/gesture-control)). Apple itself lists hands near the image edges, hands seen edge-on in a "karate chop" pose, and gloves as failure cases, and notes that feet sometimes get detected as hands ([WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/)). The fifth problem is **macOS integration**. A grant made while the app is running needs a restart, "Mission Control ignores synthetic Ctrl+arrow", and a crash mid-drag can leave the mouse button held down ([Bhavyashah20](https://github.com/Bhavyashah20/gesture-control)). Missing actions such as right-click, scroll, drag and zoom fill out the issue lists.

The "goofy" or gimmick feel is the problem that has actually killed products. Reviewers called Pixel 4's Soli "a glorified gimmick" and complained about "the short list of use cases" ([Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/google-failed-soli-pixel-4a); [Tom's Guide](https://tomsguide.com/opinion/pixel-4-motion-sense)). Leap's forums described control as "spastic" ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2013/08/29/176613/leap-motions-struggles-reveal-problems-with-3-d-interfaces/amp/)). Kinect was discontinued for lack of good content ([ZKM](https://extinction.zkm.de/events/kinect-discontinued/)). The counter-example is Vision Pro, where gaze is ignored until a pinch registers and the hands rest in the lap ([Gaze+Pinch analysis](https://medium.com/antaeus-ar/design-principles-issues-for-gaze-and-pinch-interaction-a95e251169ae); [MacRumors forum](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/these-gestures-are-how-you-control-apple-vision-pro.2392259/page-4)). Users who liked Soli liked it for narrow, hands-busy actions such as skipping songs and silencing alarms. What people say they want follows the same pattern: presentations and media control ([Show HN: Zesture](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24098874)), pinch semantics for click, drag, scroll and zoom ([Conductor](https://github.com/dev-talix/conductor)), calibration of the camera-to-screen mapping (NonMouse #16), on-device processing, and **per-user sensitivity**. Motor-impaired study participants said explicitly that sensitivity "need[s] to be customizable" ([Frontiers 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computer-science/articles/10.3389/fcomp.2021.751455/full)). Direct user quotes about CPU, heat, battery and camera privacy could not be gathered (see the gaps below).

## The technical recipe: Vision first, two filters, normalized pinch with hysteresis

**Perception engine.** Apple Vision's `VNDetectHumanHandPoseRequest` and MediaPipe's Hand Landmarker both output the same 21-point skeleton. Vision is built into macOS, gives each point its own confidence and, since 2021, reports **chirality** (left or right hand). Its known limits are no depth output, best results under about 3.5 m from the camera, and poorer results in extreme lighting ([WWDC21 10039](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10039/)). Setting `maximumHandCount` to 1 keeps latency down, and pinning `revision` prevents behaviour changes when the SDK updates ([WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/)). MediaPipe publishes latency figures, **about 12 ms on GPU and 17 ms on CPU on a Pixel 6** ([MediaPipe guide](https://ai.google.dev/mediapipe/solutions/vision/hand_landmarker)), and adds relative z, which makes "push to click" feasible. On macOS its Metal GPU delegate has failed in at least one report ([issue #5568](https://github.com/google-ai-edge/mediapipe/issues/5568)). Neither engine has a published hand-pose benchmark on an M-series Mac. An M4 should comfortably handle one hand at 30–60 fps, but that needs measuring. The sensible approach is to prototype with Vision behind an engine-agnostic interface (21 points plus confidence plus handedness), so that MediaPipe or a Core ML port ([hugging-mac/mediapipe-hand-coreml](https://huggingface.co/hugging-mac/mediapipe-hand-coreml)) can be swapped in for comparison.

**Camera.** Enumerate `device.formats` and pick an `activeFormat` whose frame-rate range covers 60 fps if the built-in camera offers one ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/capture-device-formats.md)). Request roughly 720p buffers, drop late frames, and pass `CVPixelBuffer`s straight to Vision. **Center Stage is a hidden enemy**, because its panning and zooming crop moves an absolute mapping even when the hand is still. Take control of it with `centerStageControlMode` and disable it, and warn the user if that fails ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/centerstagerectofinterest.md); [Nonstrict](https://nonstrict.eu/blog/2025/studio-display-camera-fails)). Face-driven auto exposure is controllable, which matters when the hand crosses the face ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/isfacedrivenautoexposureenabled.md)). Exposure locks are reportedly leaky ([Forums 780406](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/780406)). A competing vendor, Reincubate, claims Apple does not let apps change Continuity Camera's frame rate ([Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/computing/camo-18-takes-on-continuity-camera)). The iPhone 13 Pro is still worth testing as a better-lit, repositionable sensor, for example mounted lower to look down at a hand resting near the desk.

**Smoothing.** Use the **1€ filter**. Set `beta = 0`, tune `min_cutoff` (around 1 Hz) until a still hand stops jittering, then raise `beta` in factors of 10 until fast movements stop lagging ([Casiez](https://cristal.univ-lille.fr/~casiez/1euro); [DFKI docs](https://dfki-interactive-machine-learning.github.io/multisensor-pipeline/Documentation/modules/signal/filtering)). One hand-pose wrapper defaults to `min_cutoff = 1.0` and `beta = 0.02` ([MyoGestic](https://nsquaredlab.github.io/MyoGestic/how-to/post-process-output/)). Collabora found the 1€ filter too laggy for XR hands ([Collabora](https://test.www.collabora.com/news-and-blog/blog/2022/05/31/monado-hand-tracking-hand-waving-our-way-towards-a-first-attempt/)), so the key design move is **two signal paths**: a heavily smoothed cursor and a lightly smoothed pinch distance, so that clicks are not delayed. Add a small dead zone so the cursor can be held still for a click. Apple's own samples use no filter at all; they gate points on confidence above 0.2–0.3 and require **3 consecutive frames of evidence** before a state change ([WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/)).

**Mapping.** Map the central 50–70% of the frame to the screen, because Vision degrades at the frame edges ([WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/)). Drive the cursor from a stable point such as the index knuckle (MCP) or the palm centroid rather than the fingertip, because the fingertip moves during a pinch and makes the cursor jump just as the user clicks. Also freeze the cursor, or roll it back a few frames, at pinch onset. Popular open-source hand mice map the fingertip absolutely, and none was found that implements clutch or relative mapping ([LyraPointer](https://github.com/petehsu/LyraPointer)). That gap is the opportunity: a **relative "air-trackpad" mode with acceleration**, so that 10–15 cm of hand travel covers the screen.

**Pinch and gestures.** Apple's pinch-to-draw sample uses a fixed 40 px threshold, 3 frames of evidence and an `unknown → possiblePinch → pinched → possibleApart → apart` state machine. It buffers points during the "possible" states so stroke starts are not lost ([WWDC20](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2020/10653/)). Ultraleap uses **separate pinch and unpinch thresholds** (hysteresis) ([Ultraleap docs](https://docs.ultraleap.com/xr-and-tabletop/xr/unity/plugin/features/pinch-detector.html)). Combining the two gives a distance between thumb tip and index tip divided by hand size (wrist to middle MCP), with "on" below about 0.3, "off" above about 0.5, and 2–3 frames to enter or exit. These starting values are unsourced and need tuning. Distinguish click from drag by duration and movement. Use a thumb-to-middle pinch for right-click, a two-finger pose plus vertical velocity for scroll, and a two-hand pinch for zoom. For high-level gestures, Apple gates on confidence above 0.9 and adds a "Background" class to absorb non-gestures ([WWDC21](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2021/10039/)).

**Robustness.** If the hand disappears for 1–3 frames, coast on the last position. **After about 300 ms of loss, release any held button.** On reacquisition, reset the filters and block clicks for about 200 ms. Lock onto one hand using chirality and its last position. Reject frames with implausible jumps in bone length, because Vision's joint estimates "sometimes … can be very wrong" ([FLECT](https://cloud.flect.co.jp/entry/2021/09/21/084902)). To save power on the fanless Air, run at full rate only while a hand is engaged, drop to 10–15 fps when idle, and stop capture when paused. No published power or thermal measurements exist, so measure with Instruments.

## macOS plumbing decides whether it feels native or broken

**Input injection.** Mouse move, click, drag and scroll are fully supported through Quartz Event Services (`CGEventCreateMouseEvent`, `CGEventCreateScrollWheelEvent`, `CGEventPost`) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coregraphics/quartz-event-services.md)). Scrolls created in pixel units read as smooth scrolling in most apps ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coregraphics/cgscrolleventunit.md)). Setting the continuous flag plus scroll and momentum phase fields makes them feel like a trackpad ([WebKit 232805](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=232805); [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coregraphics/cgmomentumscrollphase.md)). True multitouch is a different matter. **macOS has no public API for injecting pinch, rotate or swipe trackpad gestures.** WebKit's test harness uses a private CGEvent type 29 for them ([WebKit SPI](https://webkit.googlesource.com/WebKit/+/54591b58a50e66aaad868f829cfce9d38eafed7b/Tools/TestRunnerShared/spi/CoreGraphicsTestSPI.h)), and no source confirms that it still works on macOS 26. "Touch-like" on a Mac therefore means pointer emulation, with zoom mapped to Cmd+scroll or Cmd+=/−. Spaces switching should use AppleScript or the Mission Control key, because synthetic Ctrl+arrow is ignored there. The private gesture path should sit behind an experimental flag. `AXUIElementCopyElementAtPosition` can drive hover highlighting and "magnetic" target snapping to offset jitter, but AX calls sporadically fail even when permission is granted ([Forums 727984](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/727984)).

**Permissions and signing.** The app needs Camera permission (`NSCameraUsageDescription` plus the `com.apple.security.device.camera` entitlement under hardened runtime) and Accessibility permission, because PostEvent appears as "Accessibility" in System Settings ([Apple DTS, Forums 820594](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/820594); [Forums 700714](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/700714)). Input Monitoring and Screen Recording are not needed unless the app listens to real keystrokes or composites screen captures. The trap that bit NathN07's users is that **ad-hoc signing pins the grant to a cdhash that changes on every build**. System Settings still shows the app as enabled while TCC silently rejects it ([nickboy](https://nickboy-github-io.pages.dev/posts/tcc-cdhash-trap/); [OpenClaw](https://docs.openclaw.ai/platforms/mac/permissions)). Sign every build with one stable certificate. Detect the state where System Settings shows the app enabled but the preflight check returns false, and guide the user to remove and re-add it. Ship with Developer ID and notarization outside the Mac App Store, because a developer reported a **Guideline 2.4.5 rejection for using CGEvent.post** ([Forums 820594](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/820594)). Revoking Accessibility while an event tap is active can freeze input ([Forums 844416](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/844416)), so keep a global kill-switch hotkey (Carbon `RegisterEventHotKey`, which needs no permission) and stop posting events the moment trust disappears. Python prototypes inherit permissions from Terminal or the IDE; macOS 15.4 broke pyautogui until Terminal was enabled ([pynput issue 424](https://github.com/moses-palmer/pynput/issues/424)). That rules Python out for shipping.

**Overlay.** Use one borderless, transparent, non-activating panel per screen, with `ignoresMouseEvents = true`, `canJoinAllSpaces` and `fullScreenAuxiliary`, at a high window level ([SuperDimmer](https://superdimmer.pages.dev/blog/building-crash-proof-overlay-windows-macos-screen-dimming.html)). Forum reports disagree on whether `.screenSaver` level or a `.mainMenu`-level NSPanel stays visible over other apps' full-screen Spaces ([Forums 826308](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/826308); [StackOverflow mirror](https://kiwix.lema.org/stackoverflow.com_en_all_2023-11/questions/36205834/allow-an-nswindow-nspanel-to-float-above-full-screen-apps)), so test both. Ink belongs in this overlay as vectors. Exporting the ink alone needs no permission; compositing it with the screen needs Screen Recording.

## The better version: a Swift air trackpad with a clutch, honest feedback and modes

The design lessons from Vision Pro, Meta Quest and Ultraleap converge. **Separate targeting from commitment, keep hands low, amplify small motions, make targets forgiving and show state continuously.** Apple's HIG says that holding the arms up "for even a little while can be physically tiring", that custom gestures should never be the only way to do something, and that the interface should "indicate when a gesture isn't available" ([Apple HIG: Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures.md)). Meta calls the index-thumb pinch the most accurate selection gesture and stresses strong audio and visual feedback, since hands get no haptics ([Meta](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/hands-ui-best-practices/)). Ultraleap recommends a proximity glow as fingers approach a target and fading visuals as the hand nears the edge of the tracking zone ([Ultraleap](https://docs.ultraleap.com/xr-guidelines/Getting%20started/design-principles.html)). The app should translate these into an explicit **engage state** (open palm held in the zone for about 250 ms; hand down or a fist disengages, like lifting a mouse). It should also have a **cursor that tells the truth**: hidden with no hand, a dim ring when the hand is seen, a solid ring when engaged, a ring that shrinks with pinch progress, a filled dot plus a click sound when pressed, a trail while dragging, and amber when confidence is low. The cursor-state sequence is a design proposal, not a tested result.

The proposed architecture has six stages on one serial pipeline, with the UI on the main thread:

| Layer | Responsibility | Key choices |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | AVCaptureSession, built-in or Continuity Camera | 720p, 60 fps if available, Center Stage off, late frames dropped, adaptive idle rate |
| Perception | `HandTracker` protocol → 21 pts + confidence + chirality | Vision default (`maximumHandCount` 1–2, pinned revision); MediaPipe/Core ML plug-in for A/B tests |
| Signal | Plausibility checks, confidence gating, dual 1€ filters, dead zone | Cursor path heavy, gesture path light; filter reset on reacquire |
| Interaction | Engage/clutch state machine; pinch hysteresis; click/drag/right-click/scroll/zoom; dwell option | Relative mapping with acceleration (absolute optional); cursor freeze at pinch onset; 300 ms loss → mouse-up |
| Output | CGEvent poster; scroll with phases and momentum; shortcut fallbacks; optional AX snapping | Stable `CGEventSource`; trust watchdog; private gestures behind flag |
| Shell | Menu-bar app, per-screen overlay, onboarding, calibration, settings | Developer ID + notarization; kill-switch hotkey; practice sandbox; profiles per camera and lighting |

**Modes** keep the product out of gimmick territory. Pointer mode is the general air trackpad. **Draw** mode uses the fingertip itself, treats a held pinch as pen-down (the most reliable webcam pen signal, since its author found a learned pen-up classifier "too difficult" to run in real time ([air-drawing](https://github.com/Haixing-Hu/air-drawing))), trims about 50–100 ms of hook from each stroke's ends, smooths after the stroke, and offers optional shape snapping. A later SkyCanvas-style "stylize" step could run Stable Diffusion on PyTorch MPS. On 16 GB that should be feasible but slow, an inference that was not measured. **Present** and **Media** modes expose a few large, distinct gestures. **Accessibility** mode adds dwell click, either-hand support, sensitivity sliders and large cursors, and coexists with Head Pointer and Alternate Pointer Actions ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-in/guide/mac-help/unac899/27/mac/27)). **Onboarding** grants Camera, then Accessibility, each with a live status check. It then shows a camera preview with the skeleton overlaid to set up the zone and lighting, and runs a calibration: hold an open hand at rest, pinch three times to set per-user thresholds, and trace a comfortable box to set the gain. A practice sandbox follows before system-wide control is switched on. Ship a **dry-run mode** that shows what the system "thinks" without acting, which one builder found necessary for trust ([Bhavyashah20](https://github.com/Bhavyashah20/gesture-control)).

## Conclusion

The gap in this market is not tracking accuracy. Vision and MediaPipe are both good enough, and an M4 has headroom to spare. The gap is interaction design and system plumbing. Every Mac tool found either maps a jittery fingertip absolutely to the screen with no clutch, or is too new to have been tested by users at all. A product that treats the hand as a **trackpad you can lift**, keeps the arm resting, and tells the user exactly what state it is in would beat every competitor on the dimensions users actually complain about. The second insight is to be honest about scope. Precision will stay below a trackpad's, so the winning pitch is "great for drawing, presenting, media and hands-busy moments, with assistive options", not "throw away your mouse". The evidence has gaps: there are no published Mac latency, power or Vision-vs-MediaPipe hand benchmarks. The first engineering milestone should therefore be a measurement harness on this exact MacBook Air, not a feature list.

## What to do next

**Try AirTouch first.** Confirm which AirTouch you meant. If it is the Neural Lab one, it won't run on the Mac. Install the airtouchapp.com app; it needs macOS 14+, and you should check the trial terms before subscribing, since the price could not be retrieved. If you would rather not pay, install Pawvis as the free baseline, and NathN07/AirTouch as the free Vision-based app that uses the AirTouch name. Grant Camera and Accessibility, and turn off Center Stage in Control Center. Test with the built-in camera and with the iPhone 13 Pro through Continuity Camera, ideally mounted lower so it looks down at a resting hand. While testing, keep notes on these questions:

- Does the cursor jitter when your hand is still, and does it lag when you move quickly?
- Does the cursor jump when you pinch?
- How often do clicks fire by accident, for example while talking with your hands or on a video call, and how often do intended clicks get missed?
- How long can you use it before your arm or shoulder tires?
- How does it behave in dim light, against a backlit window, with a busy background, or with a second person in the frame?
- What happens when your hand leaves the frame in the middle of a drag?
- Can you hit small targets such as window close buttons and menu items?
- Do scroll, right-click, zoom and Spaces switching work in Safari, Chrome and an Electron app?
- How does it behave on a second display and over full-screen apps?
- Did you have permission problems, and did you need to restart?
- What do Activity Monitor CPU and battery figures show over 20 minutes?
